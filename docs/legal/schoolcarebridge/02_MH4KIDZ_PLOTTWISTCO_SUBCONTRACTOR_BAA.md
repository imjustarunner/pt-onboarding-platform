## 1. Purpose and covered information

The parties enter this Agreement in connection with the services in Schedule A. “PHI,”
“Breach,” “Unsecured PHI,” “Security Incident,” “Designated Record Set,” “Required by Law,”
and “Secretary” have their meanings under the HIPAA Rules, 45 CFR Parts 160 and 164.
PHI here means PHI created, received, maintained or transmitted by Recipient on behalf of
Principal for those services, including electronic PHI. This Agreement does not classify
FERPA education records as PHI or authorize a disclosure that otherwise requires consent.

## 2. Permitted processing and limits

Recipient may use or disclose PHI only to perform Schedule A under Principal's documented,
lawful instructions, fulfill this Agreement, or as Required by Law. Recipient shall not
use or disclose PHI in a way that Principal could not lawfully authorize under HIPAA.
Recipient shall apply the minimum-necessary standard where applicable and restrict access
to authorized persons with a service-related need. Incidental access is not permission for
independent use. Recipient shall notify Principal if an instruction appears unlawful and
pause that processing pending resolution, except where law requires the processing.

The parties do not authorize sale of PHI, targeted advertising, independent fundraising,
unrestricted cross-customer data aggregation, general-purpose AI/model training, or use of
PHI to promote another entity's services. De-identification or analytics beyond performing
the contracted service requires a separate written instruction and applicable safeguards.
No vendor may independently reuse identifiable records under a general website license.
Psychotherapy notes and specially protected substance-use records are excluded from routine
school coordination; processing them requires a separately approved lawful scope and any
additional required authorization or agreement. No recording or transcription is authorized
merely by signing this Agreement.

## 3. Safeguards and Security Rule

Recipient shall comply with the Security Rule provisions applicable to business associates
for electronic PHI and maintain appropriate administrative, physical and technical safeguards.
It shall maintain a documented risk assessment and risk-management process; workforce
confidentiality and training; individual accounts; access approval and timely termination;
appropriate authentication; access and security logging; incident response; backup/recovery;
and secure disposal. Schedule B records the agreed technical controls and any approved
limitations. Encryption at rest and in transit and multi-factor authentication for privileged
access are proposed contractual controls; the parties must verify implementation before
live PHI access. Exceptions require written risk assessment and agreed compensating controls
and cannot waive applicable law.

Recipient shall not weaken a material protection or materially expand locations, access or
processing without informing Principal and resolving the change under the service agreement.
It shall preserve evidence relevant to an incident and protect logs against inappropriate
access. Requested audit evidence shall not expose other customers' data, credentials or
security-sensitive materials beyond what is reasonably needed to assess compliance.

## 4. Incidents and breach reporting

Recipient shall notify Principal's designated incident contact without unreasonable delay
and no later than 24 hours after discovery of a suspected or confirmed unauthorized access,
use or disclosure affecting Principal's information, or a material Security Incident affecting
its confidentiality, integrity or availability. A preliminary report may identify facts still
under investigation. Recipient shall investigate promptly, mitigate harm where practicable,
cooperate with containment and preserve relevant evidence.

For a Breach of Unsecured PHI, Recipient shall additionally provide the notice required by
45 CFR 164.410 without unreasonable delay and, under this proposed contract, no later than
five business days after discovery. Neither investigation nor incomplete information permits
Recipient to delay the initial notice. Supplement reports as information becomes available.
Include known affected individuals, data categories, discovery/event dates, what occurred,
containment and corrective actions, and a contact for further information. Applicable legal
deadlines remain controlling if shorter; a contractual deadline never extends the HIPAA limit.

Routine unsuccessful scans or blocked attempts that do not result in unauthorized access or
material impact may be reported in an agreed periodic summary, but only if Schedule B identifies
those categories. This exception does not cover a suspected breach, compromised account or
material service incident. Principal directs notices to individuals, regulators and schools
unless law independently requires Recipient to notify them. Recipient shall coordinate and
shall not obstruct any legally required report. Each party retains its direct legal duties.

## 5. Requests, amendments and accounting

Recipient shall assist Principal in satisfying individual access, amendment, restriction,
confidential-communication and accounting obligations applicable to the services. Requests
received directly shall be referred promptly to Principal unless law requires a direct
response. Recipient shall provide available information or an export within ten business
days of Principal's request, or sooner when reasonably necessary to meet a communicated
legal deadline. It shall incorporate or make available required amendments as directed and
keep the information needed for disclosure accountings. Record locations, formats and request
contacts are specified in Schedule A. Principal decides the validity of a request unless a
law independently imposes that decision on Recipient.

## 6. Delegated duties and regulatory access

To the extent Recipient performs a Privacy Rule obligation on Principal's behalf, Recipient
shall comply with the requirements applicable to that obligation. Recipient shall make its
relevant internal practices, books and records available to the Secretary as required to
determine HIPAA compliance and cooperate with lawful regulatory inquiries. This Agreement
does not prevent either party from communicating with a regulator.

## 7. Subcontractors

Before a subcontractor creates, receives, maintains or transmits PHI on Recipient's behalf,
Recipient shall bind it by a written agreement imposing the applicable restrictions,
conditions and safeguards at least as protective as this Agreement, including Security Rule
obligations for electronic PHI. Recipient remains responsible for its subcontractor commitments.
Maintain an inventory of the relevant providers, functions and processing locations; make it
available to Principal and notify it of material changes. No live PHI may be enabled with a
vendor merely because a vendor's product description says it is HIPAA-compatible. Required
agreements, account settings and actual usage must be verified.

## 8. Principal's responsibilities

Principal shall supply lawful instructions, identify restrictions or changes in permissions
that affect processing, and limit information sent to the agreed scope. Principal retains its
own notices, authorizations, disclosure decisions and workforce-access responsibilities.
Neither school affiliation nor a shared owner constitutes an authorization to release records.
Each party shall promptly notify the other of changes needed to keep instructions lawful.

## 9. Term, suspension and termination

This Agreement takes effect on the last authorized signature and remains in force while
Recipient holds PHI under the services. Principal may suspend affected processing immediately
where reasonably needed to protect information. Principal may terminate this Agreement and
the affected services for a material violation. Where cure is appropriate, Principal may
allow a written cure period of up to 30 days; it need not permit continued unsafe processing.
The right to terminate for material breach is not restricted by an inconsistent general
service term. Transition assistance shall preserve lawful record access and confidentiality.

## 10. Return, destruction and retained information

On termination or at Principal's instruction where applicable, Recipient shall return or
destroy PHI, including copies held by subcontractors, if feasible. Proposed operational
schedule: export within 30 days and remove active-service copies within 30 days after
Principal confirms receipt; protected backups expire within 90 days unless a different
schedule is completed in Schedule B. These periods must be verified and agreed before signing.

If return or destruction is infeasible or retention is Required by Law, Recipient shall
identify the affected information, reason, retention period and access restrictions in writing.
Protections continue for retained information; uses and disclosures are limited to the reason
making return or destruction infeasible or legally required. Retained copies are not available
for ordinary product, marketing or analytics use. Provide a completion/exception certificate
on request. Record retention duties cannot be evaded by closing an account.

## 11. Relationship to other agreements and changes

This Agreement supplements the identified service agreement and controls a conflict concerning
PHI protection. It does not itself authorize fees, create a treatment relationship, appoint
an employee, waive individual rights, or give a school general clinical-record access.
Amendments require authorized written agreement, except that mandatory law controls immediately.
The parties shall work in good faith to implement necessary legal updates. No website change
silently amends this signed Agreement. No third party is made an intended contract beneficiary
except to the extent required by law. Counterparts and lawful electronic signatures are permitted.
