# Bank feeds, eligibility scheduling and agency fees

Status, September 25, 2026: the Billing Workspace Settings cost planner is implemented. It is a calculator, not an invoice or live automation. It uses entered account-wide volumes, counts secondary policies separately, compares monthly/weekly/before-visit checks, and separates processor cost from proposed platform revenue. No settings are persisted and no payment or eligibility request is made by the planner.

Existing foundations: manual Claim.MD eligibility requests and dated coverage reviews; EFT evidence tracking; Stripe Connect payment methods supporting application fees; agency subscription invoices. Background eligibility scheduling and a durable eligibility-request usage ledger are now implemented (migration 1488; activation below). Agency service agreements, metered claim/eligibility charges, invoice attachment and card application fees are implemented behind deployment activation (migration 1489). A limited, opt-in bank deposit verification workflow is implemented (migration 1490). ERA usage metering and claim-level ERA posting are **not yet implemented**. Existing application-fee support does not mean fees have been configured across all app transactions.

## Account and pricing setup

Account 31985 is on Basic (owner confirmed); the owner is willing to upgrade when needed. The planner defaults to Basic. Confirm additional-TIN pricing before a plan change. Several agencies may share one clearinghouse account, but their included allowance must be counted once across the account. Allocate shared costs explicitly; never issue each tenant a separate vendor base charge unless that is an agreed service price. Preserve agency/TIN/NPI attribution for every transaction. Vendor cost and management-company price are distinct.

The planner uses the [Claim.MD public pricing table](https://www.claim.md/pricing): Unlimited $120/month with 1,000 included eligibility transactions, eligibility overage $0.02 Prime/$0.10 non-Prime; Small Volume $60 with 100 each and $0.50 overage; Basic $30 plus $0.30 each. Unlimited includes claims and ERAs. Additional Unlimited tax IDs have tiered monthly charges; enter the actual total from the contract. ERA counts are claim responses (CLP), not files. Confirm contractual rates and payer classification before invoicing.

Recommended billing design: agreed monthly agency service fee plus itemized metered services, or an explicitly contracted collections-based service fee. There is no universal standard rate. Show quantities, effective rates, credits and the price version on each invoice. Reconcile to vendor usage before finalizing charges. Do not bill an ambiguous network retry as a new completed service. Do not derive a collections fee from accepted claims or ERA amounts before the contracted collection event occurs.

For cards, [Stripe Connect pricing](https://stripe.com/connect/pricing) distinguishes models where Stripe bills the connected account versus platform-controlled pricing. Published domestic online card pricing starts at 2.9% + $0.30. The platform-controlled model also lists active-account and payout fees. Confirm which costs the actual account pays. A single percentage cannot cover every small payment because of the fixed fee. Example: $10,000 across 400 payments costs approximately $410 at 2.9% + $0.30; a proposed 1% platform fee adds $100, before any additional Connect fees. This is an illustration, not an activated rate or industry standard. Keep these agency expenses separate from patient responsibility; no automatic patient surcharge. Refunds and disputes need explicit fee reversals/credits and reconciliation.

## Background eligibility activation checklist

- Confirm payer eligibility availability/enrollment for the billing identity; verify TISI's access and check the Colorado Medicaid Provider Web Portal Other Insurance/TPL workflow.
- Configure per-agency scheduling, responsible biller, monthly check limit and account-wide allowance. Start with a user-selected frequency plus checks when coverage changes; pre-visit rules must use the actual service date. Do not interpret a cached check as verification for a different service date.
- Count each primary/secondary policy separately. Only enroll active clients with usable insurance and a valid billing location. Do not silently convert incomplete insurance to self-pay.
- Reserve budget and persist an idempotent job before the external request. Key by agency/client/policy fingerprint/service date/check period. Use one worker lease, bounded retries, visible unresolved requests and a pause control. Account for requests whose vendor charge is uncertain.
- Persist encrypted responses and new evidence in the existing review flow. New or conflicting findings require review; no automatic coverage attestation, payer reordering or release of collection holds. Absence of other coverage in a response is not proof that no other policy exists.
- Preview the expected checks/cost before enabling; show actual usage, failed checks and limits afterward. Reconcile vendor charges separately from the agency's service-price agreement.

## Chase bank connection and deposit reconciliation

[Stripe Financial Connections](https://docs.stripe.com/financial-connections/transactions) supports consented transaction access and daily refresh subscriptions. Live transactions require Financial Connections registration. Confirm that the specific Chase business account is supported in the connection flow; a Stripe payout bank or completed microdeposit verification is not automatically a transaction-feed connection. The public [transaction-feed price](https://stripe.com/financial-connections) is $0.30 per institution per account holder per month; verification, balance and ownership products have separate prices.

The owner confirmed separate Chase accounts for each agency. Use the existing Stripe integration for hosted bank-owner consent, subject to support for the actual Chase business account. No separate bank connector was added by the owner; “added” referred to usage credits.

Implementation requirements:

- Obtain consent from an authorized bank-account owner through the selected vendor's hosted connection flow. Do not collect Chase passwords in this app. Request only the read permissions needed for reconciliation, not payment initiation.
- Bind the connection session server-side to the authorized agency/account holder. Verify ownership of returned account IDs; never trust client-submitted IDs. A shared bank account needs explicit management-company permissions and agency allocation, not automatic visibility to all tenants.
- Store token references and minimal encrypted evidence only for matching deposits; never retain unrelated transactions, descriptions, account/routing numbers or bank login credentials. Bank setup and verification currently require agency admin or super-admin plus agency financial access.
- Verify webhook signatures; handle duplicate/out-of-order events, paginated backfill, refresh failures, revocation, pending-to-posted changes and voided/reversed transactions. Maintain an idempotent transaction ledger and synchronization watermark.
- Match a posted deposit to ERA payment identity, amount, currency and payee/TIN/NPI mapping using a trustworthy trace when available. Amount/date alone is insufficient. Standard transaction descriptions may omit ACH addenda/trace data; require manual review in that case. Handle one deposit covering multiple remittances without allocating the same dollars twice.
- Keep ERA posting, bank settlement and claim balances as separate records. Receiving a bank transaction must never create a second patient payment or mark all claims paid. An account's connected status must never mark every payer's EFT as verified.
- For TISI/CCHA, preserve the existing direct-deposit setup. Bank-feed access does not require replacing EnrollSafe instructions or re-enrolling EFT.

Monthly client/visit/card volumes and accepted agency service rates remain to be entered in the planner and agreements. No live bank feed, recurring eligibility checks or new service fees were activated by this change.

## Eligibility worker deployment (migration 1488)

Apply `1488_eligibility_automation.sql` before deploying the metered client verification endpoint. Set `CLAIM_MD_ELIGIBILITY_ACCOUNT_LIMITS_JSON` to an explicit map such as `{"account:31985":1000}` only after confirming the desired limit; 1,000 is an example, not an activated setting. Shared-account limits count all recorded attempts across agencies, including uncertain outcomes. Limits and usage months use UTC. A zero account limit blocks all checks; an absent account limit blocks automation but permits metered manual checks. Agency limits cover both manual and automated checks, even while its schedule is paused.

Set `CLAIM_MD_ELIGIBILITY_AUTOMATION_ENABLED=true` only after configuration is reviewed. The server then ticks every five minutes. On Cloud Run with request-based CPU or scale-to-zero, use a scheduled Cloud Run Job running `node src/scripts/runEligibilityAutomation.js` instead, or explicitly configure continuously available CPU. The job honors the same flag. Multiple workers share persisted request reservations; they cannot spend the same reserved allowance twice. Runtime activation is not part of the code push.

In Billing Workspace → Settings, select an agency, choose cadence/limit, document payer eligibility readiness and save. The saving biller is the responsible reviewer. Enroll selected clients under their billing office. Current/active clients with insurance are eligible; inactive or archived clients are skipped. Monthly/weekly checks use the run date. Before-visit checks look ahead 24 hours in persisted clinical sessions, use the session timezone and resolve its actual billing-office mapping. Visits not yet represented as clinical sessions cannot be checked by this worker. It scans 25 enrolled clients per agency per tick with a persisted cursor; size the schedule for the roster.

Changed insurance or billing identity produces a distinct request key. All attempts are reserved before transmission. Network uncertainty remains charged against the safety limit; it is not proof of a vendor fee and is not automatically billed to the agency. No automatic blind retry or positive coverage attestation occurs. Returned evidence uses the existing client insurance review and patient-collection holds. Monthly checks alone do not satisfy verification requirements for later service dates. The original Claim.MD account mode must also permit transmission.

Validation: unit tests cover period boundaries, deduplication, changed identity, revoked reviewer access, pause, both policies and unknown outcomes; isolated MySQL tests cover concurrent shared quotas, cross-account agency limits and month rollover. No real payer requests were made.

## Agency service fee activation (migration 1489)

Service prices start unset/zero. Apply migration 1489, confirm the actual Stripe fee-payer/Connect setup and review pending payment attempts before enabling `MEDICAL_SERVICE_FEES_ENABLED=true`. A platform super-admin records an accepted agency contract reference and a new immutable price revision in Billing Workspace Settings. Billing staff can read prices; providers cannot. Card fees are the platform margin, in addition to the agency’s actual Stripe fees, and are deducted from agency proceeds. Patient charges/receipts remain the original authorized amount. No automatic patient surcharge or percentage-of-insurance-collections fee is introduced. Existing management-company agreements remain in place.

The shared Stripe adapter applies the same agency card-fee quote to guardian ledger payments, legacy family payments and connected-account package checkout. Agency subscription payments and platform-account payments have no additional application fee. Quotes persist the fee, including zero; retries cannot pick up new prices or change amount/currency/account. Ambiguous references older than 23 hours require processor reconciliation. Active application fees limit those intents to cards, so an ACH payment cannot inadvertently acquire a card fee. App-initiated refunds explicitly return the proportional application fee; refunds outside the app need reconciliation in Stripe. The connected account still bears processing, dispute and refund costs according to its Stripe agreement.

Claim and eligibility service usage is recorded before its external request at the then-current accepted price. Only acknowledged claim transmission or a returned eligibility response completes a billable unit. This is the agency’s service price, not a claim that Claim.MD charges that exact unit cost. Vendor costs/allowances remain in the cost planner. Unknown outcomes and pending metering records are never invoiced automatically; reconcile them before treating them as completed. Rates and later configuration changes never reprice earlier units. Usage before activation is not retroactively charged.

Agency invoices include uninvoiced completed service units from closed UTC calendar months, grouped by month, service and original price. Invoice creation and usage attachment share a transaction and reject duplicate attachment. Existing invoice generation/payment settings still govern delivery and autopay; no additional invoice is silently created or paid by these features. An ended business agreement does not acquire new service lines. ERA transmission fees, paper claims, attachments, bank-feed fees and vendor-invoice reconciliation are not auto-invoiced by this release.

Validation includes mocked Stripe fee/refund requests and real disposable-MySQL checks for concurrent quote reuse, immutable usage prices, tenant isolation and single-invoice attachment. No live card charges or service prices were activated.


## Deposit verification deployment (migration 1490)

Apply migration 1490 and configure the family billing encryption key, Stripe secret/publishable keys in matching modes, and Stripe Financial Connections live transaction registration. Enable `BANK_FEEDS_ENABLED=true` only after setup review. In Payments, choose an agency; an authorized administrator connects its separate bank account through Stripe. Permissions request transactions only, with no payment-initiation, balances or ownership-data product. Stripe may retrieve a broader feed; this app inspects responses transiently and retains only the minimal matching deposit evidence. A connection is not EFT enrollment and does not change TISI’s existing direct deposits.

Use the existing signed platform `/api/stripe/webhook` for `financial_connections.account.refreshed_transactions`, `.disconnected` and `.deactivated` events. Events only schedule a check; the worker retrieves the current account/consent state. Configure a scheduled Cloud Run Job with `node src/scripts/runBankFeedSync.js` when web CPU is request-based or scales to zero. The opt-in server timer runs every five minutes, at most ten accounts per tick. Stripe supplies daily bank refreshes; clicking Check available evidence does not buy/request a new refresh.

Select a connected account and enter a Claim.MD ERA ID and directory page. The server re-fetches that page under the agency’s exclusive tax ID, verifies the billing-office NPI, then retrieves the selected ERA details to validate its ACH originator identity. Only minimal remittance identity is retained; raw ERA claim details, banking instructions and unrelated bank transactions are not stored by this feature. New requests restart a bounded bank-history scan; durable pagination resumes on the next tick. A single ERA cannot be allocated to two accounts, and a retained deposit cannot verify two ERAs.

Automatic verification is deliberately narrow: a posted positive USD deposit must have the exact amount, an explicit full TRN reassociation segment with matching reference **and** payer originator ID, and a posting date from the ERA payment date through 14 days later. [CMS explains payment reassociation](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/key-initiatives/burden-reduction/administrative-simplification/transactions/health-care-payment-remittance-advice-electronic-funds-transfer); matching only a generic bank ACH trace is insufficient. Many bank descriptions omit this information. Missing originator data, combined deposits, missing references, ambiguous duplicates and older/out-of-window payments remain manual. This feature does not promise universal Chase matching. Existing EFT evidence entry supports manual verification; bank matches do not automatically update payer-wide EFT status or post claims.

Previously matched transactions are checked for updates and voids. Conflicting evidence moves the verification to manual review and preserves minimal encrypted history. Separate reversal transactions without the original reference may be unverifiable and still require bank/ERA reconciliation. A verified deposit is evidence as of the last check, not a guarantee against later recoupment. Disconnect stops local imports before requesting Stripe revocation; an interrupted connection setup needs fresh consent, and completed sessions cannot reactivate disconnected accounts.

## Optional nonprofit expense module (future scope)

Keep payer-deposit verification the default for mental health agencies using QuickBooks. A separately enabled nonprofit module could relate an expense to trips, programs, grants, budgets, staff, receipts and approvals while QuickBooks remains the accounting system. Retention of broader bank activity must require separate opt-in and permissions; current billing consent does not enable it. Use explicit QuickBooks transaction references and reconciliation states rather than posting duplicate expenses. Track allocation totals against the original expense, preserve split/approval history, and separate budget commitments from actual cleared expenses. Grant eligibility and reimbursement rules require grant-specific configuration and review. This design direction is recorded, not implemented or connected to QuickBooks.

Validation: bank tests cover owner/mode/agency boundaries, completed-session replay, exact trace and originator matching, revocation failure, unrelated-data discard, duplicate/void handling and pagination recovery using a disposable MySQL database. Frontend tests cover consent, agency switching and server-bound completion; the production frontend build passes. No live bank or Claim.MD requests were made.
